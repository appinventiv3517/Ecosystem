Project overview

Seven businesses. One homepage

Bloom Holding is a diversified master developer. Its website needs to become a connected digital ecosystem.

One that communicates Bloom’s proposition while serving the needs of its different audiences.

We are evaluating the homepage to find ways to:

1

Communicate the brand proposition clearly

2

Make key user journeys easy to discover

3

Connect Bloom’s business ecosystem

4

5

Improve access to customer services

Project context in full: Bloom Holding is a diversified master developer with businesses across Real Estate, Education, Hospitality, Retail, Healthcare, Facilities Management and Landscaping (the seven arches). The current website needs to evolve into a connected digital ecosystem that communicates Bloom’s proposition while serving the needs of its different audiences. Discovery objective: evaluate the current homepage experience and identify opportunities to (1) communicate Bloom’s brand proposition more clearly, (2) improve discoverability of key user journeys, (3) connect Bloom’s business ecosystem, (4) make property and community discovery more accessible, (5) improve access to relevant customer services. Scope: homepage discovery and UX assessment only. Source: Website RFP pp. 1, 4–6.

Observations in full (preliminary, desktop only, from the live homepage captured 12 Nov 2026): 1 The first view shows the Bloom wordmark only. “Curated Community Living” appears on scroll as a heading without explanation; the master developer statement sits further down. 2 One primary action, Search Properties, with Make Enquiry in the header. No visible entry point for residents, investors or corporate visitors. 3 Who We Are, What We Do, Properties, Media Centre, Careers. Labels follow the company structure. 4 Search filters by property, type and bedrooms, with no Buy or Rent choice. Sales and Leasing are separate menu items; 13 developments are listed by name. 5 Five businesses appear as numbered tiles (Properties, Education, Hospitality, Landscape, Facilities Management). Retail and Healthcare are not shown, and the link to community living is not explained. 6 No resident or customer entry point in the header; the Bloom apps sit under “E-Services” in the footer. Mobile, Arabic and analytics review are still to come.

One homepage, multiple user intentions. The homepage needs to accommodate different audiences without requiring them to understand Bloom’s organizational structure. Key insight: different audiences arrive with different expectations. The homepage should provide clear entry points based on what visitors want to accomplish. The four pills are the four primary homepage destinations named in the RFP (p. 5): Find a Home, Explore Communities, Customer Services, About Bloom. The red italic line in each arch is that audience’s primary intention. Sources: Website RFP pp. 2, 5–6. The RFP names the audiences (buyers, investors, brokers, residents, partners, corporate stakeholders, media, employees). The four groupings and the needs listed are our working assumptions for discovery; they have not yet been validated with users. Personas are an RFP discovery deliverable (p. 6). Needs in full. Buyers & Renters: find available properties, explore residential communities, understand property details, submit an enquiry. Existing Residents & Customers: access customer services, find relevant support, access community-related services. Investors & Business Partners: understand Bloom’s business portfolio, explore its scale and capabilities, evaluate its credibility and track record. Corporate Visitors & Stakeholders: understand Bloom’s identity, explore its business verticals, discover leadership, news and company information.

Key pain points · High priority

Where does the experience fall short?

1

Unclear Brand Positioning

The homepage does not immediately communicate Bloom’s identity, scale and value proposition.

User impact

First-time visitors may not understand what Bloom offers beyond property.

2

Limited Visibility of User Journeys

The experience does not give enough prominence to the key actions visitors want to take.

User impact

Buyers, renters and residents may have to explore the menu first.

3

Fragmented Property Discovery

Sales and leasing journeys are not unified into one intuitive discovery experience.

User impact

Finding the right property can involve unnecessary, disconnected navigation.

Problems: RFP pp. 2–4 · User impacts are hypotheses to validate · Priorities are preliminary

Where does the current experience fall short? The initial discovery highlights gaps in brand communication, navigation, discoverability and audience relevance. These three are our preliminary high priorities. 1 Unclear Brand Positioning: the homepage does not immediately communicate Bloom’s complete identity, scale and differentiated value proposition. User impact: first-time visitors may not fully understand what Bloom offers beyond its property developments. (RFP p.2 A) 2 Limited Visibility of User Journeys: the experience does not give sufficient prominence to the key actions visitors want to accomplish. User impact: buyers, renters and residents may need to explore navigation before reaching their intended destination. (RFP p.2 B) 3 Fragmented Property Discovery: sales and leasing journeys are not sufficiently unified into an intuitive discovery experience. User impact: finding the right property can involve unnecessary navigation and disconnected journeys. (RFP p.3 B/C) User impacts are hypotheses, not validated user feedback. Priorities are a preliminary view for discussion.

The second half of “Where does the current experience fall short?”: three pain points we rate as preliminary medium priority. 4 Disconnected Ecosystem Storytelling: Bloom’s business verticals are not sufficiently connected through one consistent expression of Curated Community Living. User impact: visitors may not understand how Bloom’s businesses collectively contribute to its communities. The homepage should not simply introduce Bloom Holding. It should help every visitor understand Bloom and identify their next meaningful action.

The opportunity is to transform the homepage from a corporate introduction into a connected gateway to Bloom’s ecosystem. Current experience in full. Brand communication: broad corporate messaging; limited immediate differentiation. Navigation: primarily organization-led; users need to understand business categories. User journeys: important actions are not sufficiently prominent; different journeys require navigation exploration. Property discovery: sales and leasing journeys lack a unified experience. Business ecosystem: business verticals are presented as separate organizational categories. Customer services: customer-facing pathways need greater homepage prominence. Desired experience in full. Brand communication: clear positioning; evidence-led Curated Community Living proposition. Navigation: user-intent-led structure; clear and accessible navigation. User journeys: prominent entry points for priority audiences. Property discovery: unified Find a Home experience with clear Buy and Rent pathways. Business ecosystem: connected expression of Bloom’s businesses and capabilities. Customer services: easy access to relevant customer and resident services. Key takeaway: the homepage should not simply introduce Bloom Holding. It should help every visitor understand Bloom and identify their next meaningful action. Sources: “Current” summarises RFP-stated challenges (pp. 2–4) and the preliminary live-site audit. “Desired” reflects the RFP ambition (pp. 4–6). This is a direction of travel, not a layout proposal.

RFP pp. 5–6 · Areas to explore, not design solutions

The discovery findings point towards five key areas of homepage experience improvement. Sources: Website RFP pp. 5–6. The five opportunity areas map to the RFP ambition: differentiate the master brand through evidence; restructure around user intent; make the homepage the gateway to the Bloom ecosystem; unify property discovery; improve customer access to services. They are areas to explore, not UI solutions. In full: 1 Stronger Brand Introduction: establish Bloom’s identity, scale and unique proposition immediately. 2 Intent-Led Homepage Navigation: make important destinations accessible according to visitor needs. 3 Prominent Property & Community Discovery: create clearer entry points to finding homes and exploring communities. 4 Connected Ecosystem Storytelling: demonstrate how Bloom’s business verticals collectively support its community living proposition. 5 Improved Customer Access & Engagement: make customer services, enquiries and important next steps easier to identify.
